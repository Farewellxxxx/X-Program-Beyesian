# Decoupled calibration samplers: decisions and limits

This is a **sampling-efficiency comparison with a common readout**, not a verbatim reproduction of whole systems. Policies see only immutable public Observation(trial, target, realized, acceptable), a fixed PolicyConfig, an independently calibrated ExecutionBelief, and a policy RNG. They never receive a passenger, true execution parameters, hidden boundary, or another policy's observations. Trial indices start at 1 and increase. Observation subclasses and records with extra fields are rejected; valid measured execution deviations are retained. The command must belong to the shared grid, and measured acceleration must be finite within the configured physical domain.

All samplers fit the existing MonotoneBayesianLogistic to their own measured accelerations and uncorrected binary labels. The original midpoint/log-slope priors are unchanged; only quadrature grid node counts are configurable. The report is the posterior median B_tau = midpoint - logit(tau)/slope, a 95% equal-tail interval, support flag, and parameter-grid edge mass. These values are never clipped to the command domain. All-accept/all-reject cases and posterior mass near integration edges remain visible. A likelihood mismatch or sparse history can make these intervals poorly calibrated: sharing a readout controls estimation differences, not model correctness.

## Bayesian acquisition

For each command u, the public execution belief gives quadrature pairs (x_j,w_j). Conditional on each **subsequently measured** x_j, calculate q_j = E[p(y=1|x_j,theta)], and posterior B moments after y=0 and y=1. The acquisition is

    A(u) = sum_j w_j [ Var(B) - sum_y P(y|x_j) Var(B | x_j,y) ].

The implementation uses the algebraically equivalent, nonnegative expression

    gain(x) = Cov(B,p(y=1|x,theta))^2 / [q(x)(1-q(x))].

Deterministic response endpoints contribute zero. This follows the law of total variance and avoids cancellation of nearly equal variances. A cached parameter-by-prospective-measurement likelihood matrix and vector contractions avoid refitting hypothetical posteriors. Tests compare against explicit two-outcome posterior enumeration and distinguish this computation from integrating the likelihood first and keeping x forever latent. The finite execution quadrature approximates the clipped Gaussian, retaining weights of clipped nodes at domain endpoints.

`bayes_risk` averages the conditional risk reduction over public execution quadrature. `bayes_point` uses the clipped calibrated affine mean alone, with identical likelihood, prior, acquisition objective and common readout. Zero execution SD makes the two identical. Ties use the policy RNG; there are no compulsory initial measurements.

[Kontsevich & Tyler (1999)](https://www.psychophysics.ethz.ch/Downloads/protected/Kontsevich.pdf) motivate joint threshold/slope posterior inference and expected information acquisition; their Section 2.3 also discusses one-step expected posterior variance. Our Bernoulli acceptability likelihood and boundary-variance objective are explicit application choices, not a reproduction of their 2AFC detection experiment. [Oliveira, Ott & Ramos (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/oliveira19a.html) establish uncertain-input BO using distributions as GP inputs. We use their problem motivation, but implement neither their GP nor uGP-UCB theorem. The appropriate name here is Bayesian adaptive boundary estimation / BO-inspired sampling; epistemic uncertainty about B is distinct from Bernoulli response variation and execution dispersion.

## TVCG-derived UCB and secondary reward adaptation

Audit sources: Porssut et al., [*Adapting Virtual Embodiment Through Reinforcement Learning*](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2021.3057797), IEEE TVCG 28(9), 2022, supplied PDF pages 3–4, 6 and 8; original local `rlalgorithms/Algorithms/UBC_1_param.py` (read-only provenance). PDF p3 defines positive distortion gain for no break in embodiment and negative gain for a break. Page 4 specifies epsilon exploration plus Q/count confidence, learning and exploration decay, and the native stable-best-action stopping. Page 6 describes historical majority-vote correction. Page 8 evaluates detection thresholds, robustness and conservativeness in the embodiment task; those results do not establish acceleration acceptability performance.

Our primary `ucb_tvcg` preserves the reward's signed-intensity form on normalized command intensity v=(u-lower)/(upper-lower): accepted +v, rejected -v. Its expected **raw** reward is v(2p_accept(u)-1); maximizing this utility is **not** estimating the tau=.70 boundary. Map source distortion gain G in [0,10] linearly to v in [0,1]. Dividing rewards and confidence scale by 10 gives c=.2 (source c=2), preserving selection scores up to a positive scale. The common 31 evenly spaced physical commands replace the original 17 uneven gain arms. This is a domain/arm adaptation, not an exact whole-system reproduction.

Secondary `ucb_tau` uses accepted +v and rejected -tau/(1-tau)*v. Expected **raw** reward is v(p_accept(u)-tau)/(1-tau). This makes reward zero at the desired criterion, but its maximum generally lies below the crossing; it is not a criterion-consistent estimator either. A reward depending only on one binary response has expectation affine in p and cannot alone peak at an interior tau. The legacy short name `ucb` aliases primary `ucb_tvcg`; study outputs must use the explicit names.

Both variants retain the source implementation's zero initial Q, source-style Q update Q += alpha*(reward-Q), normalized c=.2 confidence bonus sqrt(log(t)/(count+.1)), and epsilon random exploration. As in source code (rather than the paper's recursively written decay), for t completed observations, the next trial uses epsilon=clip(1-log10(t/20),.01,1) and alpha=clip(.5-log10(t/40),.001,.5) when t>=1; at t=0 use epsilon=1 and alpha=.5. This reproduces the previous source loop's post-update rate scheduling, including the first decay after trial20 (exploration) and trial40 (learning). Counts and rewards attach to the **target arm**, even when measured execution differs. Both UCB variants preserve source RLExperiment.Distortion.correct_reward historical-majority sign correction for the Q reward only: increment the current target's raw sign count, use the majority sign, and retain the current raw sign on a tie. The estimator always uses uncorrected observations. The expectation formulas above describe raw rewards; corrected rewards depend on history and do not have an iid Bernoulli expectation or a standard UCB guarantee. Using identical majority correction in both variants isolates rejection-penalty sensitivity. Native best-action stability after approximately 35 trials plus 15 unchanged iterations / 100-trial cap is replaced by the shared budget and checkpoints, with no forced minimum count. The published paper and source have small indexing/decay differences; neither native stop is evidence of boundary accuracy.

## Criterion-targeted staircase

[Kaernbach (1991), weighted up-down](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03214307.pdf) motivates asymmetric steps for arbitrary psychometric criteria. With acceptability decreasing in intensity, our continuous command state starts at 1.5 m/s², initially step=.6. Accept advances step*(1-tau), reject retreats step*tau, giving expected interior state change step*(p_accept(command)-tau). Thus the zero drift criterion is tau, not 50%. Direction reversals halve the step after every two reversals, down to .15. The reversal completing a pair uses the newly halved step. Internal state is clamped at the physical domain; only the emitted command is projected to the nearest shared target (ties choose the lower command).

Finite grid projection can shift attainable criterion behavior and invalidate a literal continuous zero-drift interpretation locally. Clamping also changes drift at the edges. A persistent continuous state avoids trapping caused by rounding each update. Under execution noise, staircase sampling approaches the **command-marginal** probability criterion; it need not sample at the realized-domain B_tau. Its final report uses the shared realized-measurement estimator, not a reversal average. These are substantive differences from TVCG's four interleaved staircases, seven-reversal/20-iteration stops, and last-four-turn averaging.

## Scope

The shared main domain is [0,3] m/s² and 31 commands. Tau=.70 and delta=.15 m/s² are synthetic operational choices, not validated human tolerance or psychophysical standards. No sampler has a native stopping advantage. Report all trajectories, failures, unsupported/out-of-domain cases, exposure, error and interval coverage. Bayesian success is not an acceptance criterion. Synthetic comparative results are not human comparative evidence.
